Sam's Hope

A website redesign for a pet-assistance non-profit
  • Research
  • Information Architecture
  • UX / UI Design
Overview

Two kinds of visitor. One asks for help, the other wants to give it.

Sam's Hope is a 501(c)(3) non-profit, founded in 2013, that keeps pets and their people together through pet food and veterinary care assistance in Bucks, Montgomery and Philadelphia Counties. The redesign set out to give both visitors a clear way in.

01  The Challenge

Help was there. It was hard to reach.

Applying for assistance meant downloading a form, printing it, filling it in by hand, scanning it and emailing it back. Navigation and long passages of text made the programs themselves hard to find.

Download→Print→Fill→Scan→Submit
Problem statement

Our pet owners can't afford to take care of their pets because they have limited income. Our solution should give users the ability to financially support the user and their pet, rather than giving up their pets.

Project Context Academic project · Real-world collaboration

This project was completed as part of an academic UX program in collaboration with Sam's Hope, a nonprofit organization supporting pet owners. Our team worked directly with the organization's leadership and staff throughout the research and design process to understand their needs, evaluate the existing experience, and develop a proposed redesign.

The proposed redesign was not implemented due to the organization's resource constraints. The work presented here represents the research, recommendations, and proposed experience developed during the project.

03Users

Richard needs support for his dog. Jonathan wants to offer it.

Two personas, both in Philadelphia, shaped the site's two paths: one seeking assistance on a fixed income, one looking for an organisation he can trust with his money.

Journey

"I have to print out this form? I wish it was online."

Joseph's journey map follows a retired mechanic and his 12-year-old Labrador from the first worry to a confirmed application. The lowest point after finding Sam's Hope came at the form itself.

04  Information Architecture

Four headings, one word each.

Card sorting regrouped the site's many pages. The navigation settled on four short labels, and the homepage splits early into two paths: I need pet assistance, and I can help.

06  The Redesign

Navy, yellow and real stories.

A fixed side navigation, full-width photography and recipient stories up front. The two paths sit side by side in blue and yellow, and programs carry their eligibility in plain terms.

07Application Experience

From a printed form to four steps online.

A start page lists every document needed before anything is filled in. Then Applicant, Pet, Medical & Vet and Financials, each with a visible step marker, ending in a review page with a digital signature.
